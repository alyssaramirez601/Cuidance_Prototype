# Products and ingredients

Shown after someone picks "Products and ingredients" on U1. 3 questions and 16 answer cards. These answers change what information the result shows, not personal advice. The app never gives a safe or unsafe score.

## P1 · What would you like to explore? (Select all that apply.)

### PR-P1-01 · A product I'm considering
- **What the result shows:** Product identity; intended function; directions; the information they picked on the next questions; relevant cautions; unknowns. No purchase recommendation by default.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "A product's ingredient list explains what is included, but it does not establish how it will feel or work for you."

### PR-P1-02 · A product I already own
- **What the result shows:** The supplied product label; its intended use; selected ingredient explanations; relevant use and removal directions. No automatic replacement.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Start with the label on your product. Tell us if another listing looks different."

### PR-P1-03 · Comparing products
- **What the result shows:** A side-by-side comparison of intended function, format, fragrance information, directions, selected ingredients, and missing information.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Compare what the products are made to do before comparing individual ingredients."
- **Every skin tone:** When comparing base products, compare shade range span, not shade count.

### PR-P1-04 · Understanding what is in a product
- **What the result shows:** Ingredient roles grouped in plain language, with the original names preserved; selected ingredients expanded; formula-level limits.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Ingredients may serve roles such as cleansing, preserving, coloring, scenting, or helping retain moisture."

## P2 · What would you like help with today? (Choose one.)

### PR-P2-01 · Look up a product
- **What the result shows:** A resolved product identity, source and date checked, intended function, directions, the details they picked, and any unresolved version differences.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Cosmetics are not generally individually FDA-approved before sale; that is different from being subject to safety and labeling laws."

### PR-P2-02 · Understand what is in a product
- **What the result shows:** A short ingredient-role overview, followed by optional details and relevant cautions, not a suitability verdict.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Ingredient names do not reveal the full formula, exact amounts, or your future response."

### PR-P2-03 · Compare product options
- **What the result shows:** Matched comparison rows using the same criteria; label claims separated from independent evidence; no forced winner.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "More ingredients, a shorter list, or an earlier position does not by itself establish a better product."

### PR-P2-04 · Learn how to read a product label
- **What the result shows:** The how-to-read-a-label explainer below, using the person's label if supplied; ingredient, direction, warning, and claim fields distinguished.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Ingredient order has exceptions, and some familiar front-label terms are claims rather than standardized certifications."

### PR-P2-05 · I'm not sure yet
- **What the result shows:** The usual "not sure yet" follow-up; one optional starting task; no guessed product or ingredient.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "You can start with what the product is made for, then explore one label question."

## P3 · What information would be most useful? (Choose up to 3.)

### PR-P3-01 · Key ingredients
- **What the result shows:** A small selection of ingredients relevant to the product's purpose, their roles, evidence strength, and limits. Keep the full list available.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Key" means relevant to this explanation, not proof that other ingredients are unimportant. Ingredient properties do not automatically establish finished-product effects.

### PR-P3-02 · What the product is made for
- **What the result shows:** Intended function and application area; cosmetic versus drug classification when relevant; directions attributed to the label.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "A moisturizer, deodorant, and antiperspirant have different functions. Some products fall into more than one regulatory category."

### PR-P3-03 · Product texture or finish
- **What the result shows:** The label's texture or finish description, clearly attributed; a plain-language comparison if evidence permits.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Texture and finish depend on the complete formula; the ingredient list alone cannot promise the result."

### PR-P3-04 · Fragrance information
- **What the result shows:** Fragrance/parfum entries, individually declared fragrance-related ingredients where applicable, scent claims, and limits of the list.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "A grouped fragrance entry does not identify every component; 'unscented' is not the same as fragrance-free."

### PR-P3-05 · How to read the label
- **What the result shows:** An annotated label or text explanation covering order, names, directions, warnings, and claims.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Read the directions and warnings as well as the ingredient list."

### PR-P3-06 · Other product notes
- **What the result shows:** Relevant intended-area restrictions, product-specific warnings, storage or expiry information where present, and unanswered questions.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "These notes come from the actual label or verified rules, not assumptions about your routine."

### PR-P3-07 · No strong preference
- **What the result shows:** A short overview: purpose, selected relevant ingredients, fragrance information, and one limitation, with optional expansion.
- **Plain-language explainer:** "Start with one clear overview rather than every available detail."

## Explainer: How to read a product label

Shown when someone picks "Learn how to read a product label" or "How to read the label".

**What the app says:** "Start with what the product is made for, where it is used, and its directions. Then read the ingredient list. Ingredients are generally listed from larger to smaller amounts, but there are exceptions near the end. Ingredient names identify materials; they do not tell you the full formula or guarantee how it will feel. Front-label terms are worth checking, but they are not all standardized certifications."

| Term | Meaning | In the US | In the EU |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ingredient order | Earlier generally means a larger amount, not greater importance or effectiveness. Exact percentages usually cannot be recovered from the list. | Ingredients are generally in descending order. Ingredients at 1% or less may appear in any order after those above 1%; color additives have separate ordering flexibility. | Descending order by weight when added. Ingredients below 1% may appear in any order after those above 1%; colorants have separate flexibility. |
| INCI names | INCI means International Nomenclature of Cosmetic Ingredients: a naming system, not a safety rating. | US rules specify ingredient naming conventions and recognized references; do not assume every EU-style name is sufficient by itself for a US label. | Article 19 requires common ingredient names from the applicable glossary, or generally accepted nomenclature where no glossary name exists. |
| Fragrance or parfum | A grouped scent entry can stand for a mixture, not one ingredient. | Fragrance may generally be declared as "fragrance" rather than listing every component. | Perfume compositions are declared as "parfum" or "aroma"; specified fragrance allergens also require individual declaration under applicable conditions. |
| Fragrance-free | A claim about avoiding fragrance, not a guarantee against every possible reaction. | Truthful, non-misleading labeling rules apply; dermatologists recommend this label over "unscented" for avoiding fragrance. | General claims rules apply. EU guidance says "free from perfume" is inappropriate if any ingredient acts as a perfume. |
| Unscented | Describes an absence of noticeable scent; masking ingredients may still be present. | Not the same as fragrance-free; claims must still be truthful. | Not the same as "free from perfume"; general truthfulness and evidence rules apply. |
| Hypoallergenic | A claim about lower allergy potential, not a promise of no reaction. | FDA says no federal standard or definition governs the term. | The product should be designed to minimize allergy potential, with supporting evidence; it does not guarantee zero risk. |
| Clean | Ask what the seller means. Do not translate it into "safer," "non-toxic," or "better." | Not an FDA-approved category or federal certification. | No single "clean cosmetics" category; specific claims still need evidence. |
| Non-comedogenic | Intended to mean less likely to clog pores, not a promise that no one will break out. | A performance claim, not an FDA-issued certification. | A performance claim that needs support, not an EU certification. |
