# Every skin tone

From very deep to very light, everyone counts. Cuidance never guesses anyone's skin tone. Describing it is optional, and it only shows up when someone is matching a shade. These 15 rules keep results fair at both ends of the range.

## The 10-step reference range (based on the Monk Skin Tone Scale)

Steps 1 to 10, from very deep to very light. Used to check the spread of swatches, visuals and testers. Never used to label a person. The Fitzpatrick scale is not used: it estimates sun reaction, not skin color.

## Why this matters: the Youthforia lesson

Their deepest foundation shade was a single black pigment and looked like face paint. Nobody at that depth had tested it. An honest "no close match" does less harm than a fake match, at the deep end and at the very light end.

## The 15 skin tone rules

### ST-01 · Never guess skin tone
Evidence: Assumption · Where it applies: Every care area
Never infer skin tone, undertone or race from quiz answers, a photo, a chosen shade, hair texture or a name. Skin tone only enters a result when the person chooses to describe it.

### ST-02 · Optional depth swatches on shade paths
Evidence: Moderate · Where it applies: Makeup goal question (shade path); App copy
Only when someone picks "Complexion or base," "Finding shades or colors" or "A specific color or shade," offer an optional row of 10 swatches from deepest to lightest, plus "Between two" and "Prefer not to say." It only sorts which shades are compared first. It never labels the person, and it can be changed or cleared anytime. Use the Monk Skin Tone Scale as the reference range. Do not use the Fitzpatrick scale: it estimates sun reaction, not skin color.

### ST-03 · Depth and undertone at every depth
Evidence: Strong · Where it applies: Makeup area, makeup goal and makeup priority questions; Decisions page, gap 11
Show the same undertone comparison words (pink or red, golden or yellow, neutral, olive or muted) at every depth, from very deep to very light. Do not assume deep skin is warm or light skin is cool. Compare depth first, then color direction.

### ST-04 · "No close match" is a real result, at both ends
Evidence: Strong · Where it applies: All four makeup questions; Result template
If none of the shades fits, say so plainly and point to wider-range options. Never present "the deepest available" or "the lightest available" as a match. This is the Youthforia lesson: a deepest shade that does not match anyone does more harm than an honest gap.

### ST-05 · Check shade range before listing a product
Evidence: Moderate · Where it applies: Products branch; future product catalog
Before Cuidance lists or compares a foundation, tint, concealer or powder: record its lightest and deepest shades, how many shades sit at each end, and whether swatches are shown on real skin across the range. Judge range by span, not shade count. If a range stops short, show a note such as "This range may not include very deep shades."

### ST-06 · Red flags for deep shades (catalog and future Cuidance line)
Evidence: Moderate · Where it applies: Products branch; future own-brand line
Deep shades are built by balancing red and yellow iron oxides with black, often with little or no titanium dioxide, and sometimes violet or green to tune them. A deepest shade made from a single black pigment can look like paint. For any future Cuidance product: test every shade on people at that depth before launch. Ingredient lists do not always reveal pigment balance, so treat this as a question to ask brands, not a label rule.

### ST-07 · White or ashy cast
Evidence: Moderate · Where it applies: Skin goal question (simple routine, sun note); Look question (matte, full coverage); Body care
Titanium dioxide and zinc oxide in sunscreens and some powders can leave a white or gray cast that shows more on deeper skin. In one 2026 study, mineral sunscreen whitening was about three times stronger on darker skin, and people then applied less. Suggest tinted or sheer formulas and testing a small amount on your own skin. Never frame a cast as a problem with the person.

### ST-08 · Sun protection for every skin tone
Evidence: Strong · Where it applies: Skin results; Makeup cross-area sun note
Keep sun guidance in every skin result. On deeper skin, tinted sunscreen with iron oxides can help with visible light, which can darken spots. On very light skin that burns easily, skin cancer risk is higher, so sun guidance stays prominent too.

### ST-09 · Reactions and dryness look different on different skin
Evidence: Strong · Where it applies: Professional notes; App copy; Sensitivity question; Body care area question
A rash may look red on lighter skin and brown, purple or gray on deeper skin. Dry skin can look ashy or grayish on deeper skin. Every rash or irritation note includes the color line so people with deeper skin recognize it.

### ST-10 · Irritation can leave dark marks
Evidence: Strong · Where it applies: Sensitivity and skin goal questions; Cautions
On deeper skin, irritation from a product can leave dark spots. That makes gentle-first defaults and patch testing matter more. If someone mentions dark marks, share the AAD guidance. Never add a brightening or lightening goal they did not choose.

### ST-11 · Patch tests can change color for a while
Evidence: Strong · Where it applies: App copy (testing notes)
On deeper skin, a test spot can look lighter or darker for a while and usually fades. Add this to the testing note so people do not mistake it for damage.

### ST-12 · Every visual shows the full range
Evidence: Moderate · Where it applies: Quiz visual examples; Results; Marketing
All swatches, illustrations, coverage and finish examples, and photos show very deep, deep, medium, light and very light skin. Light skin is never the default model. Check each set against the 10-step scale before it ships.

### ST-13 · The very light end is underserved too
Evidence: Assumption · Where it applies: Makeup goal question (shade path)
Very light skin can also lack a close shade, and shades may pull pink, orange or gray. Redness also shows more. Keep "no close match" and every undertone option at the light end.

### ST-14 · Neutral color language
Evidence: Assumption · Where it applies: All app copy; shade names
No "nude" or "flesh" meaning one color. Describe shades by depth and color direction, not food or exotic names. No "brightening" or "whitening" framing unless the person chose that goal. Never rank shades or suggest a lighter result is better.

### ST-15 · Test across the whole range
Evidence: Limited · Where it applies: Week 7 validation; later testing
Recruit testers from the deepest and lightest ends, not only the middle. Use the 10-step scale to check the spread. Include the shade path in Thursday's test tasks.
